// Overview

What is this project?

The idea was simple: take a small, crescent-shaped Bluetooth tracker PCB and give it a proper home. The stock housing it came in was never meant to last. The goal was a clean enclosure — something that could hold the board, two AA battery trays wired to the module, and still close up neatly.

The design went through CAD first. I sketched the box body and lid separately, with the lid sitting flush via a lip joint. The circular module sits in the bottom half; a slot cut into the front face leaves the LED and reset button accessible without opening anything.

Wiring came next — brown and green leads from each battery holder, soldered directly to the module's positive and negative pads. Hot glue kept everything from shifting during assembly. Then the lid went on and stayed there.

The result is a palm-sized box that you can actually use. It's not flashy. It works.

// Build Process

How it came together

01
📐

3D Modeling

The box body and lid were modeled in Shapr3D on an iPad. Starting with a simple rectangular extrude, the walls got a 2mm shell, and a lip joint was added so the lid wouldn't shift under pressure.

02
🖨

Component Layout

Before printing, parts were arranged in the CAD workspace to check clearances — two flat battery trays, two ring standoffs, and the round BLE module. Everything had to fit in one layer.

03
🔬

PCB Teardown

The Bluetooth module was pulled apart to expose the board. The crescent-shaped PCB has a microcontroller, a small speaker, and a handful of SMD components. Pads for power and ground are accessible on the outer edge.

04
🔌

Wiring

Two battery holders were wired in parallel — one brown lead, one green. The polarity was checked against the module's datasheet before any solder touched the pads.

05
🔧

Soldering

A temperature-controlled iron at around 320°C. Flux on the pads first. Each joint took under five seconds once the tip was properly tinned. No bridges, no cold joints.

06
📦

Final Assembly

Components were seated into the printed shell with hot glue securing the battery trays to the base. The lid snapped over the slot cut into the front face, leaving the module's connector accessible.
